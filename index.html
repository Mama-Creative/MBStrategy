01Start here

Start here: how the whole thing works

Before any of the detail, here is the plan as a single story you can follow from beginning to end. Read this once and you will understand how everything connects and what you actually do each week. Every other section is depth on one step of this story. This is the path. The rest is the map.

The whole strategy rests on one move repeated: you write one thing, deeply, and then it travels. You are already good at the writing. What has been missing is the travelling. So follow the essay from the moment you write it to the moment it has reached people everywhere, and you will have seen the entire machine run.

The story of one essay

1. You write
Thursday, as always, you publish your Substack essay. This is the one thing you make from scratch. Nothing about your writing changes. This is the source everything else comes from, so the effort goes here and only here.
2. It becomes small
That single essay is now raw material. Its sharpest argument becomes an Instagram carousel. One line from it, or a clip of you saying it, becomes a reel. Its most analytical passage becomes a LinkedIn post. A short version becomes a Facebook note. You did not write five things. You wrote one and reshaped it four times. This reshaping is the translation system, and it is the habit the whole plan is built on.
3. It travels
The carousel and reel go to Instagram, where new people find you. The reel is the piece that reaches strangers; the carousel makes them stay. The analytical version goes to LinkedIn, the short version to Facebook. Same thinking, sent to the room that wants it.
4. It brings people back
Some of those new people come to your Substack and subscribe. Because almost nobody clicks a link out of Instagram, you use the "comment a word and I'll send you the link" trick instead, so the link lands in their messages where they actually open it.
5. It compounds
Meanwhile your name is appearing on other writers' Substack pages, because you have agreed to recommend each other. New readers arrive already warm. The essay you wrote once is now working in five places while you have moved on to next week.

That is the entire strategy in one loop. Write once, reshape, distribute, convert, compound. Everything else in this document is either the thinking behind one of those five steps or the detail of how to do it well.

How to set up a cross-recommendation
  1. In Substack, go to Settings, then Recommendations.
  2. Search the publication (for example, Israel from the Inside) and add it. It now shows on your page.
  3. Email the writer: say you have added them and ask if they will add you back. That mutual add is what puts your name in front of their readers.
  4. Start with the writers you already know. A warm ask converts far better than a cold one.
How to set up the DM-to-link reply
  1. In your reel or post caption, write: comment SUBSTACK and I'll send you the link.
  2. Use Instagram's built-in auto-reply (in the app, under professional tools) or a tool your support person sets up, so anyone who comments that word is sent the link automatically.

The mix: reels for reach, carousels and statics for depth

Reels · ~55% · reachCarousels + statics · ~45% · depth

A working ratio, not a rule. Lean the reel share up when reach is the priority; up-weight carousels when deepening. Your statics already out-engage your reels, which is right: the reel brings the stranger in, the carousel makes them stay.

What these formats actually are
  1. Reel: a short vertical video, roughly 15–40 seconds. This is the one that reaches non-followers.
  2. Carousel: a swipeable set of images or text slides, usually 6–8. One idea per slide, a hook on slide one. This is the depth format.
  3. Static: a single image or graphic. Quickest to make, good for a quote or a single thought.
  4. Hook: the first line or first second. It has one job: stop the scroll. Lead with the human moment or the surprising claim, not the background.
